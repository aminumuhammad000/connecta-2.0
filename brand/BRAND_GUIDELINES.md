## Introduction

### Overview

We help small teams do their best work. This is an introduction to who we are, what we stand for, and how we show up in the world.

### Who we are

We started with a simple belief: good work should feel effortless. Today we make tools for teams who care about craft, used by people who would rather be making things than managing them.

### What we stand for

Three beliefs sit behind everything we make and every decision in this guide.

- **Clarity over cleverness**: We say and show things plainly. If it needs explaining, we simplify it.
- **Craft in the details**: Small things add up. We sweat the spacing, the wording and the timing.
- **People first**: We design for the person using it, on their busiest day, not for ourselves.

### About this guide

This guide shows how the brand looks, sounds and behaves. Use it whenever you make something in our name, so everything we put into the world feels like it came from the same place.

### Contact

Questions about the brand, or need a file that is not here? Write to the brand team and we will help.

## Strategy

### Overview

Memorable brands are built from the inside, out. Rooted in ideas around what our company stands for, it’s this vision that drives our visual identity, who we are and what we believe in.

### Mission

Our mission is to help small teams do their best work by removing the friction between having an idea and shipping it. We build tools that stay out of the way, so the people using them can focus on what matters.

### Brand

We believe good work should feel effortless. Everything we make, from the product to the way we write, is designed to be clear, calm and considered. This idea underpins the whole identity.

## Logo

### Logos

Our logo is the most visible expression of the brand. It comes in four forms, each suited to a different amount of space. Use the lockup wherever there is room, and the symbol on its own only where the name is already clear.

### Clearspace

Give the logo room to breathe. Keep a clear area around every side, measured with the symbol as the unit, and never let type, images or other marks cross into it.

### Logo colors

The logo appears in a single color taken from the palette, chosen for contrast against its background. Never recolor it outside the palette, and never place it on a background it can’t be read against.

## Color

### Palette

Our palette is small on purpose. The first color leads and appears most; the others support it. Use them in the proportions shown here, and take values from the tiles rather than sampling them from screen.

### Proportion

Roughly how much of each color appears across a piece. The lead color carries most of the weight; the others are there to support and accent it.

### Contrast

Pairings that stay legible. Each is checked against the WCAG standard: AAA and AA work for text at any size, AA Large only for headings and large type.

### Color modes

No color system shows the full range of what we see. Each mode suits a medium: RGB and Pantone give the most vibrant color on screen and in print, while CMYK covers everyday printing with some loss of range.

## Typography

### Typeface

One family does the work across the brand. It is clean, clearly legible and made for screens, with enough character in the display sizes to feel like ours.

### Try it

Set anything in the typeface. Change the size and weight to see how it behaves at every scale.

### Hierarchy

Hierarchy tells people what to read first. We build it with size and weight, then position, capitals and color. Use these steps and nothing in between.

## Motion

### Overview

We move on one curve and three timings. Small things are quick, most moves take the base timing, and big moments are slow. Keep to those and almost everything will feel like us.

### Principles

Three ideas behind every move. Everything else in this section follows from them.

### Curve

Our signature curve. Use it for anything that moves, in place of the defaults in your tools.

### In use

Our logo, and the components every website and app has, each moving on our curve.

### Do and don’t

A few rules that keep motion feeling like us.

| Context | Do | Don’t |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Move things to show where they came from or where they went. | Animate just to decorate. |
| **Timing** | Use our durations: quick for small things, slow for big moments. | Make anyone wait for an animation to finish. |
| **Comfort** | Respect the reduced motion setting: fade instead of move. | Spin, flash or shake large areas of the screen. |

## Photography

### Overview

Our photography is honest and unstaged. We shoot real people and real places in natural light, with calm compositions and room around the subject, so every image feels observed rather than arranged.

### Principles

Three qualities make a photograph ours. Check a new image against each of them before it is used.

- **Natural light**: Daylight, from a window or outdoors. Soft shadows, no flash and no colored gels.
- **Real moments**: People doing what they actually do, in places they actually work. Nothing posed for the camera.
- **Room to breathe**: One clear subject with space around it, so type and the logo have somewhere to sit.

### Style

A small set of images that sets the tone. New photography should sit comfortably beside these.

### Treatment

How an image is shot and finished. These settings keep photographs from different shoots looking like one set.

### Do and don’t

The most common decisions when choosing or editing a photograph, with the right call beside the wrong one.

| Context | Do | Don’t |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **People** | Show people at work, looking at what they are doing. | Use models smiling at the camera. |
| **Editing** | Correct exposure and white balance, then stop. | Add filters, vignettes or heavy contrast. |
| **Type on images** | Place type on the calm part of the image. | Set type across faces or busy detail. |
| **Sources** | Commission or shoot our own photography. | Use stock images that other brands also use. |

## Illustration

### Overview

Illustration explains what a photograph can’t. Ours is flat and geometric, drawn from the shapes in the symbol and colored from the palette, and used where an idea needs simplifying rather than decorating.

### Style

A small set of illustrations that sets the style. New drawings should look like they belong to this family.

### Depth

Gradients and layering give a flat drawing depth. A gradient runs between two brand colors, or from the subject into the background, never into a color outside the palette.

### Texture

A fine grain adds warmth to large flat areas. Keep it around one pixel, apply it to whole shapes or the whole drawing, and never let it soften an edge.

### Patterns

Patterns built from the illustration shapes, in palette colors. Use them as backgrounds and bands, never behind body text.

## Iconography

### Overview

Icons help people find their way. Ours are simple, drawn on one grid with one stroke, and used to support the words around them rather than replace them.

### Icons

Our icons are designed to complement the symbol. They share the same corners, clean lines and simple shapes, so they feel like part of one family wherever they appear.

### Icon grid

Every icon is drawn on the same grid with the same stroke. The keylines guide consistent proportions across different shapes.

### Principles

A few qualities run through the whole set. New icons should follow them so they sit comfortably next to the rest.

### Sizes

Icons are drawn for a small set of sizes. Use these sizes rather than scaling freely, so strokes stay crisp and consistent.

## Language

### Overview

How we write is as much a part of the brand as how we look. Our words should feel like they come from one person: someone clear, warm and sure of what they are saying.

### Voice

Three qualities define how we sound, wherever we show up. Choose one to see what it means in practice.

### In practice

The voice is easiest to understand in real copy. Each line here shows one rule at work.

### Do and don’t

The same rules, side by side, in the places our copy shows up most.

| Context | Do | Don’t |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headlines** | Guidelines that stay up to date. | A unified ecosystem for brand expression at scale. |
| **Buttons** | Publish guide | Get started |
| **Empty states** | No colors yet. Add one to begin. | Looks like it’s a bit lonely in here. |
| **Errors** | We couldn’t save your changes. Try again. | Oops, something went wrong on our end. |

### Writing tips

A few habits keep the voice consistent, whoever is writing.

- **Write like you speak**: Read it out loud. If a sentence sounds stiff, rewrite it the way you would say it.
- **Lead with the point**: Put the most useful thing first. Background and detail can follow.
- **Cut what you can**: People are busy. If you can say the same thing in fewer words, do.
- **Say it like you mean it**: No hedging. Readers should be able to rely on what we tell them.
- **Consider the context**: A launch and an error are different moments. Keep the voice and adjust the tone.
